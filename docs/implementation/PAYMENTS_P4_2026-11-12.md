# Phase 6 P4 — operations payments dashboard, audited refunds, webhook visibility

**Status:** deployed and verified in production, inert (flag off, provider none, no rows). Release `4169624`, ledger **273** (`20261111150000`), deployment `dpl_C4ut3wMUETpAZFWpFi3b7f9UMAtk`.

## What shipped

- **Operations payments dashboard** (`/ops?view=payments`, linked from the operations queue): money-state counters (paid, payment due, checkout open, refunded, expired, provider events, overdue checkouts, priced guides), current provider and enable state, the latest 100 payments with guide, amount, trip dates, deadline, paid/refunded timestamps, provider-event count and the last ignored provider reason, a per-payment audit trail, and the ingested provider-event ledger with ignored-reason counts.
- **Audited refund action**: refund buttons on paid fake-provider payments open a dialog that refuses to submit without a 5–500 character reason; the server-side RPC still enforces every rule. A successful refund moves the payment to `refunded`, records `refunded_at`, moves the booking to `refunded`, and writes a `payment.refunded` ledger event carrying the reason, operator id and amount. The dashboard refreshes the counters and the audit trail shows the refund.
- **Read models** (all re-check `is_active_ops()` and are granted to authenticated only): `kinnso_ops_payments_list`, `kinnso_ops_payment_detail`, `kinnso_ops_webhook_events`. The API payload merges the existing status counters with the payment list and the provider ledger; `GET /api/ops/payments?paymentId=` returns one payment plus its audit events. The existing `PUT` (pause/resume) and `POST` (refund) endpoints stay unchanged in contract.
- **Webhook visibility**: accepted-but-ignored provider events (amount/currency mismatch, late event, unmapped payment) are now visible to ops with their reason, amount, provider and linked payment state, so a misconfigured or failing webhook is not silent. Signature/payload rejections are still not persisted (unauthenticated write amplification), and the ledger only shows what passed verification.
- **Dashboard UX**: the page reuses the existing operations navigation and card patterns; no secondary explanatory labels beyond what the actions need.

## Limits

- Refunds remain fake-provider only until P5 wires a real Stripe refund; the RPC rejects non-fake providers with a conflict and the UI only offers the action for fake payments.
- A refund writes the audit trail but does not yet notify the traveller or creator. Cancellation/refund messaging depends on the still-open cancellation policy decision; notify-on-refund can be added once that policy is settled.
- The payment list is capped at the latest 100 rows (`hasMore` is returned but there is no cursor UI) and the provider ledger at the latest 50; both are enough for the launch volume but will need pagination before real volume.
- The dashboard is request/refresh driven (no live subscription), and signature-failure attempts are deliberately not persisted.
- Payout accrual/receipts/invoices are still not implemented (payout tables remain schema-only); those stay in the P4/P5 scope after the owner's payout decisions.
